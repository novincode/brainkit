# Hook checks

The opening creates a reason to continue; the body must repay it.

| Mechanism | Original example | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete mismatch | “The animation was smooth. Nobody understood it.” | Show what obscured meaning |
| Consequence | “We removed one button. Support got worse.” | Supply a real supported case or label fiction |
| Visible puzzle | Show two identical-looking outcomes with different behavior | Resolve the actual difference |
| Immediate scene | “The export finished. The deadline had passed.” | Establish whose problem and why |
| Honest challenge | “Can this diagram explain it without a word?” | Actually attempt the constraint |

Ask: Can the viewer understand the question? Is the information gap specific? Is the claim supported? Is the payoff in this video? Would the opening still make sense without the thumbnail? Can a quieter opening do the job better?

Avoid “you won't believe,” empty “secret” claims, unexplained pronouns, unsupported superlatives, and fake scarcity. These are diagnostic flags, not banned strings. Titles and hooks must reflect the actual result, including an experiment that fails.

Psychological labels such as curiosity, surprise, recognition, and social proof describe possible mechanisms. They do not establish that a particular opening caused retention. Test the opening rather than assigning it a fake virality score.
