# Music and sound

Separate music, sound design, room tone, dialogue, and final mix. A single “epic soundtrack” instruction cannot direct all five.

For music: define its emotional job, energy curve, tempo feel, instrumentation, texture, rhythmic density, vocal/instrumental requirement, duration, edit points, loop/stem needs, and ending. Use descriptive musical traits instead of requesting an imitation of a named artist. Space for voice matters more than constant intensity.

For sound effects: name source/action, material, proximity, environment, duration, sync point, and tail. Distinguish literal sound from expressive punctuation. Silence can carry a turn; do not put a whoosh on every cut.

For mix: audition with the actual voice. Check intelligibility, masking, clipping, abrupt ambience changes, phase/mono compatibility where relevant, and playback on phone and headphones. Match loudness and true-peak requirements to the actual delivery destination; do not freeze one universal LUFS target. Verify current provider licenses and usage terms at generation/export time.

Save stems when supported, plus cue sheet, timestamps, provenance, and any license requirements. Do not claim listening quality from a waveform or generated filename alone.
