# Creator Visual

Make the viewer understand the important thing at the important moment.

1. Identify the story promise and what must be shown, spoken, or left unstated. Choose a visual premise that adds information rather than decorating every sentence.
2. Define a focal point, type hierarchy, semantic color roles, and repeated layout rules. Read [color and type](references/color-type.md) for details.
3. Plan shots by narrative beat. Record purpose, text, assets, voice alignment, and approximate duration. Use [motion](references/motion.md) for animation decisions.
4. Keep thumbnails legible at small size and consistent with the actual payoff. Compare concepts by clarity and relevant curiosity, not arbitrary visual complexity.
5. When rendering is available, inspect frames and continuous playback at target size with sound. Otherwise label the output a plan and list what preview must verify.

Separate craft from renderer commands. Check the chosen tool's live capabilities before implementation; do not invent API calls. Do not claim a storyboard proves readability or smooth motion until rendered. Preserve supplied brand constraints and truthfulness of depicted evidence.
